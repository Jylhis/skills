# Cover letter structure

A good cover letter is specific to this company and role. A generic one is worse
than none. Keep it under one page (roughly 250–350 words).

## Structure

1. **Opening hook (1 short paragraph)** — a specific reason for this company/role,
   drawn from Phase 2 research (a product, a value, recent news, the mission). Name
   the role. Avoid "I am writing to apply for …".
2. **Fit (1–2 paragraphs)** — connect two or three of the candidate's concrete,
   quantified achievements to the role's stated needs. Show, with evidence, why
   they can do this job. This is the body; spend the words here.
3. **Why them / why now (optional, brief)** — what draws the candidate to this
   team specifically, beyond "I want a job".
4. **Close (1 short paragraph)** — a clear, low-pressure call to action and thanks.

## Rules

- **No fabrication.** Every claim must trace to the candidate's real history.
- **Mirror the posting's language** for genuinely-held skills.
- **One page maximum.** If it runs long, cut the fit section to the two strongest
  achievements.
- Use bracketed `[PLACEHOLDERS]` for anything the candidate must confirm before
  sending: `[Hiring Manager Name]`, `[referral source]`, `[specific date]`.
- Match the candidate's authentic voice; do not inflate into corporate cliché
  (avoid "I am passionate about leveraging synergies").

## Addressing

- Use the hiring manager's name if known; otherwise "Hiring Team" or the team
  name. Do not guess a name; leave `[Hiring Manager Name]` for the candidate.
