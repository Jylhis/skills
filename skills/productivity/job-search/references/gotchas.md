# Gotchas

Non-obvious edge cases when preparing a candidate's application.

## ATS (applicant tracking systems)

- Many employers parse resumes automatically before a human reads them. Avoid
  multi-column layouts, text boxes, tables, headers/footers, and images; they
  parse badly.
- Use standard section headings ("Experience", "Skills", "Education") so the
  parser maps fields correctly.
- Match real skills to the posting's exact wording (e.g. "Kubernetes" and "K8s"
  are not always treated as the same token).
- Submit the format the employer requests (usually `.docx` or `.pdf`). When
  unsure, `.pdf` preserves layout for humans; some ATS prefer `.docx`.

## Employment gaps

- Do not hide a gap by stretching dates. That is fabrication and easy to catch.
- A brief, honest framing (caregiving, study, health, a sabbatical, contract
  work) is fine; ask the candidate how they want to present it rather than
  inventing one.

## Career changers

- Lead with transferable, quantified achievements and a short summary that names
  the pivot explicitly.
- A skills section grouped by relevance helps a reviewer connect a non-linear
  history to the role.

## Compensation

- If the posting omits a range, do not invent one in the materials. Salary is a
  conversation, not a resume field.
- If the candidate asks for negotiation help, keep numbers as placeholders until
  they confirm.

## Honesty and privacy

- Never add employers, titles, dates, degrees, certifications, or metrics that
  are not real. If something cannot be verified from the candidate's history,
  ask; do not fill it in.
- Do not include sensitive personal data that the region's norms exclude from
  resumes (e.g. date of birth, marital status, photo) unless the candidate
  requests it for a market where it is expected.

## Outbound actions

- This skill produces documents. It never submits an application and never sends
  email without an explicit, unambiguous go-ahead from the candidate.
